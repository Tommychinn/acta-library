Sales script for PDS calls (Player Development Sessions) — the player-facing session where the rep runs discovery with the student and the guided visualisation exercise. Companion resources: [linked Notion doc] and [linked Notion doc].

Call Map — PDS Call Structure

Purpose of the PDS call: (1) Qualify whether the parent/player is the right fit for the programme. (2) Get the parent to the point where they understand they need to do something to help their kid improve, that the problem is real and won't fix itself.

Part 1 — Discovery Question Areas

Section 1: Initial Greeting & Rapport

Purpose: build basic trust more from logistics, being focused on your job, and just being a normal human without coming across as salesy.

  • Hi name, can you hear me okay?
  • Great, how are you?
  • Good to meet you (pause and let them say good to meet you too)
  • Is it just the two of us for the call or is anyone else joining from your side?
  • Intro yourself concisely + background

Camera-off flow

If their camera isn't on, run this flow before moving into rapport proper. The real goal is presence and focus, not winning a camera argument.

You won’t have to use all of them. In most cases 1-3. They’ll generally be used in the order below.

  1. The ask: "I can hear you but I can't see you. Are you able to turn on your camera?"
  2. If they push back ("I can’t right now / I'd rather not" ) — push back gently, never confrontationally: "Is it that you literally don't have a functioning camera, or are you just getting to your desk?"
  3. First follow-up — the jovial call-out: "You're not walking around the house making yourself a coffee, are you? Or driving somewhere?" (keeps it light while surfacing the real issue — whether they're actually present and paying attention)
  4. If they won’t get settled, e.g. are driving/distracted: “Okay I'll give you a few minutes to get settled so we can then fully focus on this. How long do you think you'll need to [get to your desk / sort out XYZ / fill in blank] ? “
  5. If they're really strongly against putting on their camera — explain the why, calmly and without judgement: "Okay — we typically do these as camera-on conversations just because it actually helps you get more from the call. If I can see how you're reacting to certain things that I focus on, it helps me make better decisions on what's relevant to you and what I should focus on in more detail. It’s not the absolute end of the world if you can’t put your camera on, but it would genuinely help. And it also tells me you’re not walking around the house eating toast or making coffee (said with light chuckle), so we can really focus on this
  6. Focus check (use whether or not the camera comes on): "Ok well the most important thing is, are you at a desk in a position where you're able to focus on this? Are you sitting at your laptop right now?" (if they're not settled, get them settled before discovery starts)
  7. If they're clearly not going to put themselves in a situation where they are able to focus on this, judge by call volume whether or not you should reschedule to a time when the person is able to focus versus taking the call there.
    • If the call volume is low, take the call, make the most of it, and have it be a shorter call. Most likely to establish some value before rescheduling.
    • If you have a large call volume, reschedule straight away and focus on other leads.

Section 2: Frame & Awareness

Purpose: establish a level of awareness about ACTA Football, our solution, the content they've been through, and set the frame for the call - positioning of us as an exclusive academy that for talented, committed players.

Awareness

  • Ok, and so I believe you booked this call after having watched a video on our website - beyond the video, did you go through any of the resources that were shared with you?

If they’ve been through nothing else:

  • Ok, well a good place to start in that case is… what do you already know about what we do?

If they’ve been through other content:

  • Great, I guess, what stood out in particular from the content, either pre or post booking based on your child’s soccer?
    • If prospect gives detailed answer, gather player’s name, age, and level, and move straight to Section 4: Multiple Players or Section 5: Current Situation as appropriate.

Backing context

Only go into this if they’re completely green, or there is a second parent who hasn’t watched anything.

  • Great, well I’ll give an intro to who we are, what we do, and also what we do on this call
  • So at a high level, we’re ACTA Academy
    • We develop talented juniors, specifically training the part of the game that clubs generally speaking don't touch: which is… how fast a player's brain reads and makes decisions on the field
    • And so it's a neuroscience focused process, and actually the same way elite professional athletes get trained, but this kind of training unfortunately just doesn't typically exist at junior levels
    • So we bring that opportunity to juniors so they can train and develop like professionals
      • And so you remember the guy presenting the video that you watched?
    • Ok so that was Coach Garrett, the founder of ACTA
      • Everything we do in the Academy is a training process he built over 20 years of coaching
        • Developing hundreds of players to Division One College, or Pro. I think just over 30 youth national team players, and back to back ODP championship winners in the past few years
          • He’s a humble guy so doesn’t really go around shouting about this, but it’s worth mentioning so you know this isn’t just some process pulled out of thin air (say with smile/light chuckle)
            • Pause. They might ask a probing question about his background/the process. Answer concisely before moving to next language pattern about exclusivity of the academy below.
  • As you can imagine, and with any academy, there’s a lot of support that goes into it from our side so we’re quite careful who we give offers to
  • And so this call is an introduction for us to make sure it’s a fit at a high level
    • Provided it is, fantastic. We then book an evaluation call with your [child] to make sure they
      • Firstly, also want to join the Academy
      • And secondly, they meet the entry requirements and that we think they’ll really benefit from it
    • But that being said, I’d love to find out more about the player we’re speaking about today, is it your son, or daughter?
      • Follow through to Section 3: Initial Intention & Context

Section 3: Initial Intention & Context

*If the prospect already give you good context in Section 2 about their player in response to awareness questions, just gather name, age, and level the player is playing at if you don’t already have it before moving to Section 4: Multiple Players, or Section 5: Current Situation as appropriate.

Purpose: gauge where they're coming in from and what the general backing context is. You'll likely probe a little bit into the backing situation (e.g., if it's their son or their daughter, what level the child is playing at). They will likely elaborate a little bit on the challenge or the reason that they are there, which will lead you into the next section of challenge evaluation.

Initial question

2 options. Choose based on what level of knowledge you already have on the prospect’s situation:

Option 1: You already have a good idea of situation/problem - likely SDR successfully called & qualified / you spoke by text/email

  • I have a bit of context from [SDR name’s conversation with you / our conversation by text], as well as the form, but I'd like to go into more depth on [son/daughter's name]’s soccer. It seems that [XYZ] are causing [him/her] some difficulty in [his/her] game right now, and that’s really what brings you to the call?
    • They’ll likely just expand. If not, either probe into important things they said, or jump to Current Situation

Option 2: Minimal context - No notes from SDR. Small to no engagement in messages

  • So I have a very small amount of context from the form
    • Is it your son, or daughter this is for?
    • Ok can you give me a bit of context - what’s his/her name, age, and what level is he/she playing?
    • What specifically is going on in his/her game that made you think our training might be able to help?

Section 4: Multiple Players

*Only use this section if there are multiple players. Otherwise move to Section 5.

If there are multiple players, pick one to focus on first - and depending on time, possibly the only one. Loop discovery around who's most important and who they believe we're likely to help the most. Get the tie-downs around that, then restart the probing sections from 5 onwards (Challenge through to Gap, Cost of Inaction, etc.) for the next player.

Choosing the player

  • Okay so what are their ages and names and what levels are they each playing at?
  • It always makes sense to focus on one player at a time when we have these conversations. From what you've seen so far about what we do, which of your players do you believe this is likely to be most relevant to?

Then go into a normal discovery around that player (Sections 5 onwards).

Restarting discovery for the next player

You'll likely finish off the discovery round with that player by saying something like:

  • Great, I'm pretty certain that this is something that would really help [player]. Can you talk to me about [other player's name] - what specifically is happening in their game that makes you think this could help them?

Section 5: Current Situation

Challenge

Purpose: get the challenge specific and vivid in the parent's own words, find out the false solutions they've already bought, and get them to talk about their root cause theory

If they’ve already given you a bit of context about the challenge in Section 2: Frame & Awareness, or Section 3: Initial Intention & Context go straight to probing questions below

Entry Question

  • From your perspective, what is the biggest challenge in [player]’s game, or ultimately what do you think is limiting him/her from performing at his/her max?

If they say there’s no challenge, don’t believe them. It’s BS. Either they as a parent don’t see it or just think their kid is a perfect prince/princess. You need to reframe the way they look at the idea of a challenge.

  • Ok, another way to look at it, what do [player]’s coaches say are the biggest areas [he/she] can improve?
  • If [he/she] were to instantly be pushed up the next division/league level, where do you think [he/she] would experience challenges in [his/her] game versus the more advanced players?
    • What level would [he/she] have to jump up to in order to experience performance difficulties against other players?
  • What is preventing [him/her] from getting picked for the next division/league level?
  • What does [player] think are the areas that will improve [his/her] game on the field?

Probing

Detailing the problem (specifics, examples, and how far it stretches)

  • What makes you say that specifically is the biggest challenge?
  • Can you give specific examples of when you’ve seen this happen on the field?
  • How frequently is this happening?
  • Is this just in live games or also in training?
  • How long has this been going on for?
    • Oh wow, that’s a long time. Has it stayed the same, gotten worse or better over that time?
  • When [he/she]'s at [his/her] best, what does that look like in terms of on the field play, and how you see that [he/she] is feeling?
    • What percentage of the time do you think [his/her] gameplay is like that?
      • Oh wow, that’s not a lot of the time

Root cause (their theory of why — sets up the Mechanism Reveal)

  • What do you think is going on in [player]’s head when [XYZ challenge] happens, I guess what do you think is causing it?

What they’ve already tried & why it hasn’t worked

  • What if anything have you and [player] already done to try and solve this? Whether that’s 1:1 coaching, mental training
    • Considering that we’re here, and this may be stating the obvious, but I’m guessing it didn’t solve the issue to the level you both want?
  • Why do you think their coach hasn’t been able to solve this with specific guidance, and just more training?
  • How much genuinely individual, one-on-one development does [he/she] actually get from club?

Impact & future-based (what it’s costing - on the player, selection, and beyond games)

  • How does [player] react in and after these moments - does [he/she] recover straight away, or is it a bit of a snowball that once [he/she] does [XYZ mistake] it starts cropping up more in the game?
    • Hmm ok. That sounds like it’s it’s affecting [his/her] game quite a bit
  • Is it impacting [him/her] getting picked for the squad, or time spent on the field?
  • How do the coaches react to [him/her] after these moments?
  • So this is affecting [him/her] in games. What about in tryouts, how is [he/she] performing?
  • [He/She]'s [age], going into [grade]. In your experience as a parent, how much time does [he/she] realistically have to close this gap before the recruiting picture starts to form?
  • It seems like [player] naturally loves playing the game, but right now clearly isn’t enjoying it as much as he could or used to. The first thing that comes to my head is that 70% of kids quit playing club level before they’re 13, with the top reason that it stops being fun. How bad is [his/her] change in enjoyment of the game?
    • Do you think his enjoyment will be solved if [he/she] starts playing better on gameday/stops doing [XYZ mistake]?

Section 6: Positive Affirmation

Purpose: Demonstrate that you actually give a shit and are on their side to help them. You have to say this authentically caring about the fact that you’re going to be able to help their kid progress past something that is limiting their enjoyment and development in the game.

If you do this right it opens them up to spill emotionally with you being able to go deep in any area of the conversation. Later in objections this trust and them truly feeling you’re on their side enables you to push them hard because they feel it’s coming from a place of genuinely wanting to help.

Use this when you’ve reached a point of understanding what the root cause is of the problem that is affecting the player’s game.

It serves as a transition before you reveal the mechanism.

  • Well I’m happy we’re having this conversation. It seems like [XYZ] that [player] is experiencing is [Causing Negative Impact], which is obviously tough on [him/her] because of [XYZ]. But I’m glad we’re speaking because this is a very common problem/situation that we help players with

Example scenarios — blanks filled in

  • Well I’m happy we’re having this conversation. It seems like the second-guessing that Mia is experiencing is costing her minutes on the field, which is obviously tough on her because of how well she plays in practice compared to what the coaches get to see on game day. But I’m glad we’re speaking because this is a very common problem that we help players solve.
  • Well I’m happy we’re having this conversation. It seems like the freezing on the ball that Jake is experiencing is snowballing into more mistakes as the game goes on, which is obviously tough on him because of how much pressure he puts on himself every time he steps on the field. But I’m glad we’re speaking because this is a very common situation that we help players with.
  • Well I’m happy we’re having this conversation. It seems like the anxiety that’s causing Sofia to play it safe and pass backwards is stopping the coaches from seeing what she can actually do with the ball, which is obviously tough on her because of how much work she’s put in outside of team training. But I’m glad we’re speaking because this is a very common situation that we help players with.

Leave a pause for them to articulate a response

Section 7: Mechanism Reveal

Purpose: Introduce the high level idea of what’s causing the problems experienced by their player.

Reveal part 1:

  • Can I share what I think is actually the root cause of this?
  • Because what you’re describing may have symptoms of a lack of confidence, anxiety, or freezing on the ball. But it’s not actually caused by these things, and it’s not a talent problem either. What you're watching is a decision-making speed problem. When [player] gets on the ball, [his/her] brain is running a conscious calculation: pass, dribble, shoot, where's the pressure, where's the space. And that process just simply takes too long. And so the game moves on before [he/she] is able to react, and that’s when [XYZ] happens. And that's not [his/her] fault. It’s just that nobody's ever trained that part of [his/her] game.
    • Does that match what you’re seeing?

Reveal part 2:

  • So you don’t solve the confidence, the freezing, or the anxiety by working on them directly. You solve them by training this decision-making speed underneath. Getting [player]’s brain to read the situation and pick the right option automatically, at game speed, instead of having to consciously calculate it in the moment, sometimes fast enough to make the play, sometimes not.
    • And when they make the right decision 50% more frequently, and faster, this compounds into their perceptions of their ability. And so the confidence problems, anxiety, and freezing start going away. And in turn the better performance leads to them getting more game time, more exposure, and improving at a faster rate, so it all snowballs.
      • Have any of [his/her] coaches, or anyone ever talked it through with you the root causes of these kinds of performance issues that way before? (the answer is always no — that's the point)
        • (when they answer no):
        • Again, that’s pretty common, most coaches just don’t know that this is what’s actually going on, which is also the reason why it so rarely gets treated correctly, and so the problem just persists

If you didn’t give context on our achievements in Section 2: Frame and Awareness, Backing Context:

  • We've been coaching and developing youth players for 20 years leading through to ACTA. We've produced over 30 players who've gone on to national teams. Two have turned professional. Hundreds have gone on to play at the Division I level. The reason those numbers exist isn't because we found better athletes. It's really simple and it’s just because we actually trained this part that gets ignored.

Section 8: Goal

Purpose: Get the dream both from the perspective of the player, but also the parent. You need the parent’s emotions attached to the success.

So I have a pretty clear idea of what’s limiting [player]’s game right now. But I’d also like to talk about [his/her] aspirations:

  • I guess what does [he/she] talk about that excites [him/her] about achieving in the game. Both shorter term over let’s say the next 6 months, but also longer term - is [he/she] wanting to play in college, go pro?
    • Do you think [he/she] has the ability to go pro/get a college scholarship if [he/she] were to really dedicate [him/herself] to it?
    • And if [he/she] is able to do that, how stoked do you think [he/she] is going to be?

Parent goal preframe - you each need to write your own here. I’ve given an example for Brad. I’ll help you each with this.

Brad:

  • So I know what drives me in my coaching, I’ve got a few kids of my own whose teams I all coach. I don’t know if mine are going to go pro or get scholarships, but I love seeing them progress and the enjoyment they get from the game when it’s going well, and I want to help them as much as I can on that journey, and who knows, maybe they will go pro, right

Sarah:

  • TBC

Lead directly from rep specific preframe to the actual question:

  • Parent goal preframe
  • And from your perspective, what would you like/love to see in [his/her] game as a parent?
    • That’s awesome. I would love for us to be able to make that happen.

Section 9: Gap

Purpose: articulate the gap between where [player] is right now and where [he/she] and the parent want [him/her] to be and why that gap exists referencing the root cause

  • Well as I said before, I’m happy we’re having this conversation. Your [son/daughter] has a great opportunity in [his/her] game and right now [XYZ problem] is really limiting [his/her] enjoyment and progression in the game. And this is exactly what we help players solve.

Section 10: Future Pace Negative Outcome

Purpose: double down on the fact that the problem isn’t going to get solved by doing nothing. Ask first question, followed by one of the second two.

  • And well, I think I likely know the answer to this already, but do you think [he/she] is going to get past this just carrying on training in the way [he/she] is with her current coach and club?
  • Well potentially a kinda hard question, but what do you think tryouts look like for next season if [he/she] carries on playing like this?
  • Well potentially a kinda hard question, but what do you think her game looks like in 12 months if [he/she] carries on playing like this?

Section 11: Why Now

Purpose: get rid of time related objections by tying the prospect down to the need to act now

  • Ok so obvious question. [XYZ problem] has been going on for [Y] amount of time. Why is now the moment that you’re actually looking to get some real help with with as opposed to [Z] months ago when it first started, or even a few months from now? I guess what changed?

Part 2 — Close-Out Sequence

Section 12: Mini Pitch

Purpose: give just enough context on what the coaching actually is that the pricing conversation makes sense. Don’t teach the full method here — that’s what the evaluation call is for. 60 seconds max.

  • So I’ve already talked a bit about what we do at a high level and the science behind it. But to expand on how we actually apply our training method
    • It’s 1:1 guided coaching, so [player] works directly with a coach, using [his/her] own game film
      • So rather than generic drills, the coach takes real moments from [his/her] own games, the decisions [he/she] made, the ones [he/she] missed, and trains [his/her] brain to read those situations and pick the right option automatically, at game speed
    • As I touched on it’s the same neuroscience based process that professionals get trained with, just brought to junior level
    • And we measure it. So you’re not guessing whether it’s working, you see [his/her] decision speed and accuracy before and after
  • Now I’m deliberately keeping this high level. On the evaluation call we’ll run through in full exactly how it all works, and [player] will actually experience some of the coaching live, so you won’t just be taking my word for it
    • Does [player] already have game film that we would be able to review in coaching sessions?
      • If yes
        • Ok great. That makes it easier for us to directly diagnose what’s going on in [his/her] game
      • If not
        • Ok no worries. Would you be able to film [his/her] games if we were to start so we have the game footage to work with?

Section 13: Pricing Qualification

Purpose: qualify that the family can actually make the investment before booking the evaluation call. We don't know their financial situation at this point, so run the flow with everyone and read how much friction comes back. Go deeper where needed. If useful, normalise against what they're already spending on club, trainers, and tournaments each season.

If qualified move to Section 15: Decision Maker and Evaluation Call Frame

  • And have you invested in private coaching or any paid development programmes beyond club fees for [player] before?
  • Ok and so you're aware, there is an investment to join the ACTA Academy. This isn’t me asking for that right now of course, but we do have to make sure it would be financially possible before booking an evaluation call with [player]
  • There are different tiers which above anything just relate to length of coaching but range from $3,000 to $6,000
    • It sounds like you feel this is something that is going to help your [son/daughter]. One of the concerns is I want to talk to him and make sure he’s a good fit. We don’t want to just assume it, but I don’t want to do that unless you feel like this is a solution that’s going to work for you.
      • Not only time commitment, but the financial commitment as well. I would hate to get [him/her] on a call and have [him/her] all excited about it only to find out it’s not something you’re willing to pay for.

Go into this if getting pushback with them thinking you're asking them to buy

  • Ok and I'm not asking you to invest at this point, this is more for an idea of if this were to be exactly the thing that gets [player] past [XYZ] and to where you've both said you want [him/her] to be, is that something that you would be able to invest in??

"Do you have payment plans?"

  • It’s really dependent on a family’s situation. In the vast majority of cases it’s up front but we’re also human and understand families have cash flow to manage
    • Are you asking that because it would literally be impossible for you to make that kind of investment in one, or would it just make things a bit easier for you to break it up? Again this obviously isn’t me saying to start right now
      • If they say they have the money move to next section
  • Ok, well look, I’d like to see if we can help you with this. From what you’ve said [player] sounds like they could really benefit from this. But I don't want to book you through to the evaluation call if you definitely can't afford it as that's no good for anyone.
    • Can you just be super open with me about what you have access to right now that you could invest in [player]’s soccer development?
      • If have nothing then DQ

Section 14: Disqualification Flow

Purpose: disqualify them respectfully.

Financial DQ

  • Ok, so to be very transparent, based on what you've mentioned about resources, this won't be something that we can help you with right now
    • It’s a shame that’s the case as [XYZ problem] that [player] is experiencing is exactly what we typically help with.
      • Something that may be able to help regardless of [player] joining the academy, is Coach Garrett's free content on our website. We have a lot of resources there that help players improve their decision making ability on the field and will help player get past [XYZ]
  • Thank you for your time, all the best, etc…

Player fit DQ (rec league, under 9, or the player just isn't motivated to improve)

  • Ok so to be very transparent, based on what you've mentioned about [player]'s situation right now, this isn't something we would be able to help with
    • We focus more towards [committed club-level players / players a bit further along in their development]

Culture fit DQ (if the parent is someone we can't have in the Academy community)

  • Ok so what I'll do at this point is go back and speak to the team
    • We have a limited number of spaces for who we can onboard and to be very transparent, only a small percentage of players get through so please don't be disappointed if we can't onboard [player]
      • If we do select [him/her] then I'll reach back out to schedule the evaluation call to run through how this would work
        • But regardless I'll reach out to you to let you know

Section 15: Decision Maker & Evaluation Call Frame

Purpose: frame the next call as an evaluation, like an admission into the academy, and use that frame to get everyone who matters on it: both parents involved in the decision, and [player]. Compulsory for them all to be there. Assume compliance.

Framing the evaluation call

  • So the next step from here is the evaluation call. That's where we actually meet [player], and confirm that everything we've discussed so far about [his/her] situation is something we can genuinely help with, that [he/she] will be committed and able to do the training.
    • And so a really important part of the call is actually a live coaching example so you’ll all see the outcome firsthand of the training and get a feel for how it will help.
      • Pause and let them respond with buy in to the call
  • And so, are you sole parent or guardian for [player] or does [he/she] have another parent or guardian who would have to be involved in a decision if [he/she] were selected for the academy and you were to invest in joining?

If there's another decision maker/spouse

  • Ok great, so in that case it would be the four of us for the evaluation call. Off the top of your head do you know their availability for the next 2 or 3 days?

If they don't have access to the availability

  • Hmm, ok. It is really important for both parents/guardians to be there, as aside from just decision making, if both parents are hyped about the process they motivate their kids more to get better outcomes in it. So what I’d suggest, let's book in a time that you, [player] and I can definitely make, as opposed to going back and forth on email a ton
    • If you can then confirm in the meantime that [partner] can make it, we can either keep the event or reschedule to another time

[Player] is a required attendee, not optional . The evaluation frame doesn't work without meeting the player, and deals where the player isn't on the call fall apart at far higher rates.

Section 16: Booking the Call

  • Book the call with the soonest availability, but making sure they have enough time to go through the resources
  • Limit apparent availability, only giving them a couple of options to choose from
  • Make sure the time works for [player] to be there too, not just the parent
  • Never "I'll email you the links" — an undated exit converts at zero. Lock a specific date and time before hanging up
  • IMPORTANT: book the event in an INCOGNITO WINDOW using THEIR TIMEZONE
    • If you don't do this GHL pulls cached data from other prospects and messes up the CRM. They also will get pre call texts listing the wrong time for them

The script:

  • So I'm pulling up the calendar here. What timezone are you in?
    • Ok, so I have 9am or 10am Central on Tuesday, or 11am on Wednesday. Do any of those work?
      • Great and I have [XYZ@gmail.com] as your email address. Is that the best one for you?
        • And will your [partner/spouse] be with you in person at that time for the call or will they be joining from work etc? In which case I would need to add them to the event.
          • Ok that's booked for you. Could you please check your email to make sure you received it?
            • Great, and one more basic admin thing. Could you check the event is actually added to your calendar? Different inboxes sometimes automatically accept and other cases don’t so events get missed.
          • Oh wow, so you're actually booked with [closer name]. [He/She] works really closely with Coach Garrett and our players and families. [He/She] doesn't normally take these calls so it's quite rare to be speaking with [him/her]

Section 17: Hype for Resources

Ridiculously important to get them to go through the resources. You'll just get them to sell themselves on the idea so they close in shorter calls at higher rates.

Give them personalised recommendations. You can only do this when you actually know what the resources are so you need to watch & read them until you know them in depth.

  • Before we jump off, there's something really important to talk through. Did you go through any of the resources on the website beyond the video you booked through?
