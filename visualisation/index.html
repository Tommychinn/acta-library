Visualisation Exercise

Black — say this Blue — backing context, don't read aloud [Boxed] — swap in their detail

Set the position and the whole script resolves — nothing stays generic. The scene in step 2 and the two questions in steps 4 and 5 swap to that position, and every bracket in the fixed steps fills with that position's own wording. Kit colours are the only blanks left to fill live; type them in as the player answers.

Pick the exact side. Sided positions have a variant per side of the field — never run a winger or a fullback generically. For the central positions (6/8, 9, goalkeeper) the directions are the canonical set; if the player places things on the opposite side, flip every left and right as you go.

Player's position

Directions

you show = dribble to your left (shoulder drop) → defender shifts to your left → real action = pass to your right, to the teammate the player placed there.

you show = switch to your right (to your right center back) → the 9 turns toward the switch → real action = step forward into the middle with the ball.

you show = switch to your left (to your left center back) → the 9 turns toward the switch → real action = step forward into the middle with the ball.

you show = inside pass to your right (back to your midfielder) → the right back jumps inside → real action = touch past them down the left line.

you show = inside pass to your left (back to your midfielder) → the left back jumps inside → real action = touch past them down the right line.

you show = pass down the left line (to your left winger) → the presser overruns to the line → real action = chop inside to your right, to your 6.

you show = pass down the right line (to your right winger) → the presser overruns to the line → real action = chop inside to your left, to your 6.

you show = layoff straight back to your midfielder → the center back steps forward onto your right shoulder → real action = let it run to your left and spin left, off their blind side.

you show = throw out to your right (to your right back) → the striker's run bends to your right → real action = release underneath, in the middle, to your 6.

Step-by-step breakdown

Part 1 — The visualisation exercise

  1. Ask permissionFixed

    "I wanna give you an example of how it works. That'd be okay?" / "Would love to show you how it works if you would be open to it."

    • Optional confidence prime: "Take a little bit of bravery on your part, but I think you're gonna do great. I know you're smart — you know how I know you're smart? Because they kept moving you back to positions that require you to be smarter."
  2. Set the scene
    • Position Fixed "You've played [position], right? … imagine you're playing [position]." / "Is that a normal position for you to play?"
    • Kit Fixed "What do you wear for your colored jerseys?"
      • "So you're in [their color] and the opponent is in [other color] — it'll just make it easy for us to visualize."

    Scene — swaps with position. One beat at a time, check in between.

    • "I want you to imagine that you are facing in toward the middle of the field, about a third of the way up. Your center back is just above the D. The goal you're defending is to your right, the opponent's goal is to your left."

    • check in

    • "The center back receives the ball, she takes a touch towards you, and now she's gonna pass the ball forward to you."

    • check in

    • "You're gonna take a quick check and you see there's a little bit of space — the next defender is at least three, four yards away. So you're gonna let the ball go across your body onto your left foot, because that's your upfield foot, and now you're gonna be turning and facing forward."
    • "Imagine your goalkeeper has the ball at their feet, just outside the 18. You're the left center back, and you've dropped wide to your left, about level with the corner of the box, to receive. Their striker — the 9 — is about ten yards away and starting to move toward you."

    • check in

    • "Your keeper plays it into your feet."
    • "Imagine your goalkeeper has the ball at their feet, just outside the 18. You're the right center back, and you've dropped wide to your right, about level with the corner of the box, to receive. Their striker — the 9 — is about ten yards away and starting to move toward you."

    • check in

    • "Your keeper plays it into your feet."
    • "Imagine you're playing left wing, high and wide, hugging the left touchline."

    • check in

    • "Your midfielder has the ball in the middle of the field and plays it out to you. You've got a bit of space, so you receive it facing forward — facing the opponent's goal. Their right back is three, four yards off you, closing you down."
    • "Imagine you're playing right wing, high and wide, hugging the right touchline."

    • check in

    • "Your midfielder has the ball in the middle of the field and plays it out to you. You've got a bit of space, so you receive it facing forward — facing the opponent's goal. Their left back is three, four yards off you, closing you down."
    • "Imagine you're playing left back. Your center back has the ball and plays it across to you, out to the left."

    • check in

    • "Their right winger is running at you to press, and your left winger is ahead of you, up the left line."
    • "Imagine you're playing right back. Your center back has the ball and plays it across to you, out to the right."

    • check in

    • "Their left winger is running at you to press, and your right winger is ahead of you, up the right line."
    • "Imagine you're playing as the 9. Your midfielder is driving forward with the ball, and you check toward them to receive it into your feet with your back to goal."

    • check in

    • "Their center back is tight on your back, leaning on your right shoulder — you can feel them there."
    • "Imagine you've just caught a cross and you've got the ball in your hands."

    • check in

    • "Their striker is jogging toward you to close you down. Your right back is wide on the right, and your 6 is showing underneath in the middle."
  3. Check in (use throughout — never advance until they confirm)Fixed

    "With me so far?" · "Are you able to picture this in your mind?" · "Do you feel like you can see this in your mind?"

  4. Option 1 — the predictable play

    Option 1 — swaps with position. Let them predict the defender's reaction.

    • "As you start to receive that ball and face forward, what do you expect the defender (about four yards away) to do?" · "Imagine you see a passing option on either your left or your right — where do you see it?" · "If you take your touch toward the right and begin to pass, what's her reaction gonna be?"
    • (steps in to their left, toward your right, to close the passing lane — the pass gets more difficult.)
    • "As it arrives, you open up toward the left sideline like you're gonna play down the left line. What's that 9 gonna do?"
    • (presses your outside [left] shoulder and shows you down the left line — your forward options narrow.)
    • "As it arrives, you open up toward the right sideline like you're gonna play down the right line. What's that 9 gonna do?"
    • (presses your outside [right] shoulder and shows you down the right line — your forward options narrow.)
    • "If you push your touch straight down the left line, what's that right back gonna do?"
    • (shows you to the left touchline — the lane down the line gets smaller.)
    • "If you push your touch straight down the right line, what's that left back gonna do?"
    • (shows you to the right touchline — the lane down the line gets smaller.)
    • "If you open up and get ready to play the obvious pass down the left line, what does their winger do?"
    • (angles the press toward the left touchline to cut off the line — the lane closes.)
    • "If you open up and get ready to play the obvious pass down the right line, what does their winger do?"
    • (angles the press toward the right touchline to cut off the line — the lane closes.)
    • "If you receive it and just lay it straight back to your midfielder, what does that center back do?"
    • (stays touch-tight on your back — safe, but you never turn, never threaten.)
    • "If you look straight at your right back and wind up to throw it out to the right, what does the striker's run do?"
    • (bends to your right to cut off that lane — the throw gets riskier the longer you wait.)
    • If they stall, narrow to a yes/no Fixed "Step in and try to close that space?"
    • Name the problem Fixed "That's gonna make the [pass/throw] more difficult — the space gets smaller."
  5. Rewind → Option 2 — the deception
    • Rewind Fixed "Let's rewind the tape in your head. Same ball, coming to you just like before."

    Option 2 — swaps with position. Let them predict what the defender does differently.

    • "But now imagine you let the ball run across your body onto your left foot and you drop your left shoulder, as if you're gonna dribble to the left. What do you think the defender will do differently in that circumstance?"
    • (bites to your left, following the shoulder drop — and the space opens back up on your right, where your passing option is.)
    • "Rewind. Same ball. This time you turn your hips in to your right and open your body like you're gonna switch it across to your right center back. Now what does the 9 do?"
    • (turns in toward that switch — and the middle opens for you to step into.)
    • "Rewind. Same ball. This time you turn your hips in to your left and open your body like you're gonna switch it across to your left center back. Now what does the 9 do?"
    • (turns in toward that switch — and the middle opens for you to step into.)
    • "Rewind. This time, as they get close, you swing your hips to your right like you're gonna pass back inside to your midfielder. What do they do now?"
    • (jumps toward the inside pass, to your right — and the left line opens up for you to attack.)
    • "Rewind. This time, as they get close, you swing your hips to your left like you're gonna pass back inside to your midfielder. What do they do now?"
    • (jumps toward the inside pass, to your left — and the right line opens up for you to attack.)
    • "Rewind. This time you open your hips like you're playing down the left line — and then chop it inside, to your right, toward your 6. What happens to the player pressing you?"
    • (overruns toward the left line — and the inside lane, to your right, opens.)
    • "Rewind. This time you open your hips like you're playing down the right line — and then chop it inside, to your left, toward your 6. What happens to the player pressing you?"
    • (overruns toward the right line — and the inside lane, to your left, opens.)
    • "Rewind. Same ball. This time you shape your body like you're gonna lay it off again — but instead you let it run across your body onto your left foot and spin off to your left, over their blind side. What does the center back do when you show them the layoff?"
    • (steps forward onto your right shoulder to squeeze it — and space opens behind them, on your left, for the turn.)
    • "Rewind. This time you keep your eyes on the right back and shape like you're throwing right — as the striker's run bends that way, who's now open underneath?"
    • (the 6, in the middle — you moved the presser with your eyes and your shape, without touching the ball.)
    • Optional Fixed "Do you do many feints like that in the game?"
  6. Space recognitionFixed
    • "When they go [the way you showed them], where is new space being created for you?"
    • "And what's [where the space opens] — remind me?"
    • Payoff: "So where's the space now? And who created it?"
  7. Control question & principleFixed
    • "So now in this moment, who is in control of what's happening here?" → "You're the actor. He's the reactor."
    • State the principle: you had information he didn't (asymmetry), so you could manipulate his movement with something as simple as a shoulder drop.

    Position-specific extra

    • "Your look was the feint — same rule as everywhere else on the field."

Part 2 — The debrief Fixed — every position

  1. Clarity check

    "When we were going through this example, was it very easy for you to picture in your mind what was going on?" / "Were you able to paint that picture in your head?"

  2. Real-field anchor

    "Quick question — what field were you on?"

    • "Isn't that funny? It wasn't some random made-up field."
  3. Friction probe

    "So what was easy about it? What was hard?"

  4. Agency reveal

    "While we were talking, who was deciding what happens next? Was it me, or was it you?"

    • If they say "you": "Was I? Think back — did I tell you, or did I ask you what was going to happen?"
    • "Your brain had to solve those problems — and they're not complicated, are they?"
  5. Motor imagery reveal

    "Have you ever heard of something called motor imagery?" → "Would you be surprised to know that your motor cortex — the part of your brain that controls muscle movement — was actually performing those actions while we talked about it?"

    • "It's pretty trippy — and almost everyone is surprised by it if they didn't already know that's what's happening. I encourage you to look it up."
  6. Bridge to the program

    In the real program this is done with the player's own game footage — "hundreds of repetitions" of a game moment in ~45 minutes — plus the results promise: "players improve 20-30% faster than their teammates… you'll notice a difference within the first couple of weeks."

  7. Transition to close
    • Value/impact checks:
      • "What do you think of that?"
      • "How do you feel about that? You feel like that's something that would be impactful in your game?"
      • "Do you think that would help you deal with those kinds of moments better in real live games — being able to work out how to solve them in practice like this?"
      • "So how pumped are you about this helping you [play with more confidence / do XYZ in your game]?"
    • Micro-commitment: "It's about 30-45 minutes a week of video work — is that something you'll be able to do?"
    • "Do you have any questions for me on how this would work for you?"
    • Handoff: "Do you want me to chat with your mom a little bit and talk about what the next step is?"

Example Flow 1 — Maddie (13-year-old midfielder/centre back)

Source: recorded call — exercise 09:07–13:15, debrief 13:15–17:30. Condensed but verbatim.

Setup & scene (V1–V3)

Coach: So let me give you an example of how our framework works, if that's okay. Take a little bit of bravery on your part, but I think you're gonna do great. I know you're smart — you know how I know you're smart? Because they kept moving you back to positions that require you to be smarter… Just imagine for a second that you are playing as a central midfielder. What do you wear for your colored jerseys?

Maddie: We have light blue, white, and then black.

Coach: So just imagine that you're wearing your black jerseys, and the opponent is wearing white — that way we can create sort of a visual… The center back on the left-hand side receives the ball, she takes a touch towards you, and now she's gonna pass the ball forward to you. Are you able to picture this in your mind?

Maddie: Yeah.

Coach: You're gonna take a quick check and you see there's a little bit of space — the next defender is at least three, four yards away. So you're gonna let the ball go across your body onto your left foot, because that's your upfield foot, and now you're gonna be turning and facing forward. Do you feel like you can see this in your mind?

Maddie: Yeah.

Option 1 — the predictable play (V4)

Coach: As you start to receive that ball and face forward, what do you expect the girl in white — about four yards away from you right now — to do once you turn and face forward?

Maddie: I'm not sure.

Coach: (narrowing to a yes/no) You think she might pressure you? You think she might start to step forward to pressure you?

Maddie: Oh, yeah. She would start to pressure me.

Coach: In your mind's eye, I want you to imagine there is a passing option either on your left or on your right — tell me where you see that passing option.

Maddie: I see it, like, on the side, like, on a wing… my right side.

Coach: So what happens then, as she's pressuring you, if you take your touch with your left foot toward the right and you begin to pass? What's her reaction gonna be?

Maddie: She'd shift to the right.

Coach: Step in and try to close that space?

Maddie: Yeah.

Coach: Perfect. So that's gonna make the pass a little bit more difficult, right? The space is gonna get smaller.

Option 2 — rewind & deception (V5–V6)

Coach: Let's reset this. Let's rewind. Center back has the ball, she plays it to you, it's running across your body… but now just imagine you let the ball roll a little bit farther — have you ever done in a game like a feint, where you drop your shoulder as if you're gonna dribble?

Maddie: Kind of. I feel like something that I struggle with in games is doing moves. Like, I'll practice them, but I just can't perform in games.

Coach: (affirm the pain, promise, continue) Yeah — that's one of my favorite things to help with. We're gonna do so great with that… and this example is gonna help you understand it. As the ball is going across your body and you drop your shoulder to go to the left, what do you think she might do differently compared to the other example?

Maddie: She would try to cut off the space to the left.

Coach: Right. When she goes to the left as you drop your shoulder, where is new space being created for you?

Maddie: The right.

Coach: And what's on the right? Remind me.

Maddie: The goal.

Coach: The player you wanted to pass to is there. Yes?

Maddie: Yeah.

Coach: (principle) So in this scenario, you knew you wanted to go to the right, but by initially taking that movement to the left, you were actually able to control — we say manipulate — the movement of the defender and create the space you wanted in the first place.

Debrief (D1–D6)

Coach: I wanna ask you some questions about what we just did. When we were going through this example, was it very easy for you to picture in your mind what was going on?

Maddie: I feel like it was easy and hard at the same time.

Coach: Quick question — what field were you on?

Maddie: What field was I on? … I was at, like, the field that I practice at.

Coach: Isn't that funny? It wasn't just some random made-up field — it was an actual field that you practice at. So what was easy about it? What was hard?

Maddie: It was easy to understand how I'm receiving the ball, but it was harder to tell where the goals are and which way I should be turning — looking for the pass.

Coach: It's very easy to visualize in your mind if you have the right structure for visualizing. The second thing I wanna ask: while we were talking, who was deciding what happens next? Was it me, or was it you?

Maddie: I got to choose which way to pass the ball… I feel like it was more me.

Coach: Yeah — because really, all I did was ask you questions. This is something called experiential learning: when you get to experience it, those neural pathways are built much more quickly and they're much stronger… But what I think is really cool: how many things in your soccer life are 100% about you, 100% of the time?

Maddie: It's never just about me — I can be out of position, or I go help and then my position is open… it's very much a team thing.

Coach: I haven't even told you the coolest part yet. Are you ready? What we just did is a form of what is called motor imagery… would you be surprised to know that your motor cortex, the part of your brain that controls muscle movement, was actually performing those actions while we talked about it?

Maddie: (laughing) Yeah!

Coach: It's pretty trippy. Almost everyone gets the same reaction… I encourage you to look it up. We use your actual game moments, your decisions in games, so your brain gets to practice this moment over and over and over… players that do our program improve about 20-30% faster than their teammates, and you will notice a difference within the first couple of weeks.

Transition: the call then moves to "What do you think of this, Maddie?" → the 1-10 readiness scale with Maddie, then with mom.

Example Flow 2 — Aiden (2013 defender/midfielder, dad Josh on the call)

Source: recorded call — exercise 05:07–10:05, debrief 10:05–12:17. Condensed but verbatim.

Setup & scene (V1–V3)

Coach: Would love to show you how it works if you would be open to it.

Aiden: Yeah. Definitely.

Coach: So you've played midfielder, right? Center mid? I'm gonna talk you through an example where we're gonna imagine you're playing the six. You good with that? … What color does your team usually wear?

Aiden: Red, usually.

Coach: So we're gonna say you're in red and the opponent is in white — it'll just make it easy for us to visualize. I want you to imagine that you are facing in toward the middle of the field, about a third of the way up. Your center back is just above the D. The goal you're defending is to your right, the opponent's goal is to your left. With me so far?

Aiden: Yep.

Coach: He's gonna play you the ball forward. And because you checked your shoulder and you see there's a little bit of space, you're gonna let that ball go across your body — you wanna take it on your upfield foot, which in this case is your left foot. Right?

Aiden: Yep.

Option 1 — the predictable play (V4)

Coach: As you're turning, you're gonna notice that defender is probably going to be doing what? What do you expect he will probably do?

Aiden: Probably step, most likely.

Coach: Might wanna apply some pressure… So now your job is to solve that pressure. You could just turn and play negative — super easy — but we would be going backwards instead of forwards. And we wanna go forward. Why do we wanna go forward?

Aiden: Because it progresses the play… the goal.

Coach: Because that's where the goal is. Game's super simple… Now I want you to imagine that in your mind's eye you see an option to pass on either your right or your left. Tell me what you're seeing in your mind's eye right now?

Aiden: Right now I'm seeing, like, one or two wingers making a run.

Coach: Which side are you seeing the passing option on?

Aiden: The right.

Coach: As you turn and open your right hip to play that pass to the guy on your right, what do you think that opponent — the one applying pressure — is going to do differently?

Aiden: Well, he's gonna adjust his body position, right?

Coach: Yeah — is he gonna step into that passing lane and try to take that pass away?

Aiden: Yes… I mean, maybe.

Coach: He's definitely going to adjust his run based on you turning your hips. Perfect — that's all we need to know.

Option 2 — rewind & deception (V5–V6)

Coach: So let's just reset now. Center back has the ball, he plays forward, you're turning, the ball's coming across towards your left foot. You see the teammate on the right still. But your opponent has his back to that player — he doesn't know where that player is. True?

Aiden: Mhmm.

Coach: So imagine that instead of taking the ball to your right, you let it go across your body to your left, and you just sort of drop your shoulder going forward like you're gonna dribble. What do you think the opponent will do differently in that circumstance?

Aiden: Well, he would step to the left, right? But I have a feeling we'd go to the right.

Coach: When he steps to the left, where is the space being created?

Aiden: On the right side.

Coach: Right. He doesn't know you wanna pass to the right — he doesn't even know there's nobody to pass to on the left. He's reacting to what you're doing. So now in this moment, who is in control of what's happening here?

Aiden: Me.

Coach: (principle) Yeah. You're the actor, he's the reactor — simple as that… You created that space because of your action. You recognized the only three things that mattered: you have the ball, he's that distance away applying pressure, and you know where the release is. And all we did was drop our shoulder.

Debrief (D1–D6)

Coach: A couple of interesting things about this little scenario, and I wanna get your feedback. Number one — were you able to very clearly see what's going on? Were you able to paint that picture in your head?

Aiden: Yeah, I saw it pretty good.

Coach: Which is cool. But also, you were the one involved — it's what we call experiential learning. Because as we were talking, who was making most of the decisions about what was gonna happen in that scenario? Was it me, or was it you?

Aiden: It was you. You were telling me what I was gonna do, right?

Coach: Was I?

Aiden: I mean, I did it in my head…

Coach: No, no — think back. Did I tell you, or did I ask you what was going to happen?

Aiden: You did ask me what was gonna happen.

Coach: A lot of times, right? "What would happen if?" "What do you think you would do if?" So here's the really amazing part: your brain had to solve those problems. And they're not complicated, are they?

Aiden: No.

Coach: But in the game you don't generally solve those problems, because you have so many other things going on in your head… Here's the thing I haven't told you yet, and this is gonna blow your mind. The part of your brain that controls motor activity on the field — the motor cortex — was fully activated. It literally does the motions as if you're on the field.

Aiden: It's cool.

Coach: Everybody giggles when I tell them that… But even better — we're not watching somebody else play, are we? We're watching you play. It's about you 100% of the time, and it's probably one of the only things in your soccer life that will be about you 100% of the time.

Coach: You seem excited.

Aiden: Yeah — I wanna get faster at playing and stuff.

Coach: Well, you will… You're gonna start noticing differences in just a few weeks. You're gonna be a little irritated, thinking: this seems so easy — why did nobody teach this to me until I was 13 years old?

Question explainer & categories

Visualisation exercise questions

CategoryExample questionsWhat it does / how it links forward
Permission openers"That'd be okay?" · "Would you be open to it?"Gets explicit buy-in and hands the player agency from second one — which the agency reveal (D4) later depends on.
Personalisation anchors"Is that a normal position for you to play?" · "What color does your team usually wear?"Pulls real details into the scene so the player's brain builds it from memory. Sets up the "what field were you on?" reveal (D2).
Comprehension check-ins"With me so far?" · "Are you able to picture this in your mind?" · "Do you feel like you can see this in your mind?"Pacing gates — never advance until the image is confirmed. Also collects the evidence for the clarity check (D1).
Prediction questions"What do you expect the defender to do once you turn and face forward?" · "What do you think his response will be?"The first real decision handed to the player. Teaches reading an opponent's intent; every answer becomes a building block the rep never has to assert.
Option identification"Where do you see that passing option — your left or your right?" · "Tell me what you're seeing in your mind's eye right now?"The player authors the scene detail the entire payoff depends on. Because they placed the teammate, discovering the space later feels like their own insight.
Consequence questions (Option 1)"If you take your touch toward the right and begin to pass, what's her reaction gonna be?" · "Is he gonna step into that passing lane?"Establishes the problem: the obvious pass telegraphs intent and shrinks the space. Creates the tension Option 2 resolves.
Deception setup (Option 2)"Have you ever done a feint — where you drop your shoulder as if you're gonna dribble?" · "What do you think she'll do differently in that circumstance?"Rewinds the tape and swaps one action. The player predicts the defender biting — authoring the deception mechanic themselves.
Space recognition"Where is new space being created for you?" · "And what's on the right — remind me?" · "Now who can you easily pass to?"The payoff. "Remind me" forces the player to retrieve the detail they placed earlier, so the solution lands as self-discovered.
Control question"So now in this moment, who is in control of what's happening here?"Lands the principle — actor vs. reactor, information asymmetry — as the player's own conclusion. The rep only names what the player already answered.

Delivery principles

Patterns across all three calls.

  • Ask, never tell. The rep asserts almost nothing during the exercise — every building block (defender pressures, lane closes, space opens, "me") comes out of the player's mouth. The rep only names principles the player has already supplied.
  • Narrow when they stall. If a prediction question gets "I'm not sure" (Maddie) or confusion (Tristan: "Could you elaborate?"), immediately downgrade to a binary or yes/no: "You think she might pressure you?" · "Do you see a teammate on your right or on your left?" Never let the player feel wrong.
  • Affirm every answer. "Perfect." "Right." "Great." — after literally every response. Wrong-ish answers get redirected by rewinding, not corrected.
  • "Rewind the tape" is the reset mechanic. Every new option starts with "let's rewind / let's reset" and re-narrates the scene from the same starting frame before changing one variable.
  • Use their real details, then reference them back. Position, kit colour, the teammate they placed — "remind me?" turns their own details into the payoff.
  • Treat struggle admissions as gold. When Maddie admitted she can't perform moves in games, the rep affirmed ("that's one of my favorite things to help with"), promised, and continued the exercise — then had a personalised proof point for the close.
  • The parent is always watching. The exercise doubles as a demo for the buying parent — Rafi texted mid-exercise: "I could almost watch his brain trying to rewire and think differently in real time." The debrief lines about experiential learning and motor imagery are as much for the parent as the player.

Overall summary

The visualisation exercise is a Socratic two-option demo: build a vivid, personalised game scene (their position, their kit, their field), have the player solve it the obvious way and discover the defender kills the space (Option 1), rewind and have them solve it with deception and discover they control the defender (Option 2), then debrief so they realise three things in order — I could see it clearly, I made every decision, and my brain was physically training the whole time. Each stage is driven entirely by questions, so the player experiences the coaching rather than hearing about it; the debrief converts that experience into the program's three core claims (experiential learning, motor imagery, 100%-about-you); and the emotional high it creates flows directly into the temperature check and close. Typical player responses are short, affirmative, and increasingly confident — hesitation early ("I'm not sure") turning into ownership by the end ("Me." / "It was more me.") — and that visible shift is precisely what sells the parent watching.
